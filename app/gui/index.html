Characters

Every avatar you've started. Open one to carry on, or start a new one from a model.

Disk space

Every build keeps its files, run by run: about 300 MB a run. An old run's model files (.blend, .vrm) can go; its reports and logs stay, so builds can still be compared. Kept whole: the build in use, the one marked good, and any you wrote a Warudo note about.

Step 1 of 8

New character

Pick the model to turn into an avatar. Everything you decide on the later pages is saved with it as you go.

More about this page

A character is one model you turn into a VTuber avatar. Parts, joints, face and options are saved in out\<name>\character.json. To go back to one you already started, open it from Characters.

The textured model straight from Tripo (P2 works best). Recommended: in Tripo press Rig, pick "VRM 1.0 humanoid" and export as FBX. That one file holds the model, its texture and Tripo's skeleton, which usually bends better than the app's own; the app finds the skeleton by itself, also through a .glb the texture tools (matte, face patch) made from that FBX. Don't use Tripo's rigged GLB: its skeleton is broken. An unrigged model works too: the app places the skeleton.

Generate it standing in a T-pose (arms straight out) if you can: sleeves then don't fuse to the ribs. An A-pose (arms angled down) still builds.

A folder name for the build: letters, digits, _. Also the avatar's name in Warudo.

Advanced: files you usually don't have

Getting it ready runs the survey, lists the pieces and makes the face patch in one go, a minute or two. Then you mark the eyes and mouth and press Save and build. Untick to go page by page yourself.

Step 1 of 8

Model

Run the survey first: it only reads the model and changes nothing. Then go down the pages in order.

More about this page

The survey reads how the model stands, what it's made of and which options suit it. To start a different character, go to Characters.

Survey

One Blender run, under a minute. Nothing is changed. Get it ready also lists the pieces and makes the face patch, whichever of the three is still missing: after it, mark the face and build.

Tripo's rig (optional)

The same model rigged in Tripo (Rig → "VRM 1.0 humanoid" → export FBX). With it, the skeleton and body weights come from Tripo instead of the app's own guess, which usually bends better at the shoulders and hips. Without it the app rigs the model itself; that works too. A rigged FBX model is its own rig: only fill this in when the rig is a separate file. A skeleton from Mixamo or AccuRig / Character Creator is read too when you name its FBX here (not yet tried on real files: clear the field if the build stops at it).

Step 2 of 8

Parts

Say how each piece should move. Most are fine on automatic; only give a role to what should move differently from the skin under it. Every change saves right away.

More about this page

A generated model is made of separate pieces (the body, hair strands, a hat, sleeves...). Click a piece in the view or the list. Suggestions are guesses: a sleeve looks like cloth until an arm moves (the Movement page tests that).

drag to orbit · scroll to zoom · click a piece
Part of a piece for something fused into a bigger piece

A twin-tail that is part of the body: pick the piece, then paint the part with Alt+drag (Ctrl+Alt erases), or Shift+drag a box (Ctrl+Shift adds). Take all of it, ribbons and the hidden side included: whatever is left out stays on the old bone and tears away from the rest.

Step 3 of 8

Joints

Where the skeleton's joints sit. Click a ball, then drag its arrows; each move saves on its own. Blue: detected. Orange: moved by you.

More about this page

Detection gets most models right; long robes and wide sleeves can fool it (a robe hem once read as the crotch, 44 cm off). A picked joint stays picked, so you can nudge it as often as you like; Reset puts it back. Needs the piece listing (Parts).

click a ball · drag its arrows · drag empty space to orbit · Esc lets go
JointWhere

Each joint saves itself when you let go of its arrow.

Step 4 of 8

Face

Show the app where the eyes and mouth are, so the avatar can blink, talk and look around. Three steps, top to bottom.

More about this page

Left and right always mean the character's own sides (the character faces you, so their right eye is on your left).

1 · Face patch

2 · Mark the eyes and mouth

Click the highlighted point on the picture, twelve in all: four per eye (the two corners, the top and bottom of the opening, where the lashes meet the white) and four for the mouth. Drag a dot to move it. The 3D view shows where each lands on the head: the dots must sit on the eyes and the mouth (a mouth once got marked on the nose). They save by themselves once all twelve are placed.

the marked points on the model · drag to orbit
3 · How the face moves
Eye tracking

Makes the eyes follow where you look in the tracking app (Warudo: set eye movement to "blendshapes"). Blinking works either way.

Mouth

By default the mouth opens by bending the face around the marked mouth. If it barely opens, looks smeared, or the face dents when talking, redraw it.

Replaces the talking shapes (a, i, u, e, o) with drawn mouth shapes fitted to the marked mouth: a dark opening with a tongue that opens cleanly.

Also paints the model's own mouth out of the texture and draws the whole mouth on a separate patch that moves on its own, so talking never bends the face. Best for simple or chibi faces, and when the face dents.

how big the drawn mouth is against the marked one (1.3 = 30% bigger; model A used 1.3)

Step 5 of 8

Options

Switches for the whole avatar. The defaults suit most models; "auto" lets the survey decide and says what it picked. Each change saves on its own; rebuild for it to count.

Step 6 of 8

Build

Turns everything you set up into the avatar file (.vrm). Build everything runs the whole chain: a few minutes.

More about this page

Step 7 of 8

    
Movement 

    
Catches clothing that doesn't move with the body, before you find it in Warudo. Two tests, both run in
      Blender and report back. To watch the avatar move, open it on Review.

  

  

    
Pose test

      
Bends the rig the way tracking apps do (arms raised, forward, out and down; head turned and nodded) and
        lists the pieces that don't come along: a sleeve left at the ribs, a hat that stays while the head turns. It
        suggests the part role that fixed each kind of problem before. About a minute; needs a build up to “pieces”.

      

    
Physics test

      
Runs the finished avatar's hair and cloth springs, standing still and then swaying, as Warudo does
        (renders never run them). It reports hair or cloth that droops, pieces left behind by a swing, and swinging parts
        sinking into the body. Needs a finished build.

Which Blender runs the builds

      
Any Blender 4.2 or later with the VRM Add-on ("VRM format" in Blender's Get Extensions). The app runs it in
        the background with only that add-on switched on, whatever else is installed in it, so your own Blender setup is
        never touched.
