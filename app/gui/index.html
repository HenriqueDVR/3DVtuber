Characters

Every avatar you've started. Open one to carry on, or start a new one from a model.

Disk space

Every build keeps its files, run by run: about 300 MB a run. An old run's model files (.blend, .vrm) can go; its reports and logs stay, so builds can still be compared. Kept whole: the build in use, the one marked good, and any you wrote a note about.

After the app itself changed

Rebuilds every character that has a finished build, each in a scratch folder (its own files are not touched), and compares the checks with the build you marked good, or else its last build: a rule that helps one character must not quietly break another. A few minutes a character, one after the other; Stop ends it.

Automatic

Quick build

One thing at a time: the app does the work and tells you when it needs you.

    Refine it yourself optional: for when you know what you want to change
    Step 1 of 8

    New character

    Pick your 3D model and give it a name. That is all the app needs to start.

    You can switch any time (top bar), and nothing is lost: it is the same character either way.

    What happens next
    1. The app reads your model. About a minute, nothing to do.
    2. You check the face. It shows where it found the eyes and mouth; you say if that is right, or mark them yourself.
    3. It builds the avatar. A few minutes. Then you see it move, with anything worth a look pointed out.

    A .fbx, .glb or .obj of a standing character, or a finished .vrm. Best: Tripo's rigged FBX, made in a T-pose.

    Which file should I use?

    The textured model straight from Tripo (P2 works best). Recommended: in Tripo press Rig, pick "VRM 1.0 humanoid" and export as FBX. That one file holds the model, its texture and Tripo's skeleton, which usually bends better than the app's own; the app finds the skeleton by itself, also through a .glb the texture tools (matte, face patch) made from that FBX. Don't use Tripo's rigged GLB: its skeleton is broken. An unrigged model works too: the app places the skeleton.

    Generate it standing in a T-pose (arms straight out) if you can: sleeves then don't fuse to the ribs. An A-pose (arms angled down) still builds.

    A finished avatar (.vrm, VRM 1.0 or 0.x) from VRoid or anywhere else is taken in as it is: its skeleton, weights, expressions, springs and materials stay, and nothing is rigged again. The build straightens its joints for finger tracking, can add chest bounce (Options), checks it and writes a new .vrm beside it, always as VRM 1.0. For an avatar someone else made, its licence may have to be read first: the app asks.

    A character is one model you turn into a VTuber avatar. What you decide is saved in out\<name>\character.json. To go back to one you already started, open it from Characters.

    Letters, digits and _. It is the avatar's file name in your tracking app.

    Advanced: files you usually don't have

    Getting it ready runs the survey, lists the pieces and makes the face patch in one go, a minute or two. The one thing left for you is the Face page: mark the eyes and mouth and press Save and build. Untick to run each page's step yourself.

    Tripo's rig (optional)

    The same model rigged in Tripo (Rig → "VRM 1.0 humanoid" → export FBX). With it, the skeleton and body weights come from Tripo instead of the app's own guess, which usually bends better at the shoulders and hips. Without it the app rigs the model itself; that works too. A rigged FBX model is its own rig: only fill this in when the rig is a separate file. A skeleton from Mixamo or AccuRig / Character Creator is read too when you name its FBX here (not yet tried on real files: clear the field if the build stops at it).

    Try a choice both ways

    Makes this character again under another name: the same model, parts, joints, face points and paint, with its own builds. Change one thing in the copy (moving iris or sliding eyes, a bigger mouth, chest bounce) and build both, instead of writing one choice over the other.

    This character

    A new name shows in the list and inside the avatar file after the next build; its builds stay where they are. Taking it off the list deletes nothing: it can be put back from the Characters page. Deleting removes its builds, face points and paint for good; the model file you made it from stays.

    Step 2 of 8

    Parts

    Say how each piece should move. Most are fine on automatic; only give a role to what should move differently from the skin under it. Every change saves right away.

    1. Look it over. Turn the model and click pieces, here or in the list: each says what it will do.
    2. Most need nothing. If the suggestions look right, press Accept all suggestions.
    3. Change only what's off: hair, a skirt or a tail that should swing, a hat that shouldn't bend. Then Next.
    More about this page

    A generated model is made of separate pieces (the body, hair strands, a hat, sleeves...). Click a piece in the view or the list. Suggestions are guesses: a sleeve looks like cloth until an arm moves (the Movement page tests that).

    How the eyes move tracking · which eyes show · fine-tune

    Makes the eyes follow where you look in the tracking app (in Warudo, set eye movement to "blendshapes"). Blinking works either way. Eyes behind a visor, glasses or a mask: choose "eyes are covered", and only the mouth's four points are asked for below.

    one under hair or an eyepatch: mark only the one that shows. The other stays exactly as it is (no blink, no eye movement), and its place is taken as the mirror of the shown one across the mouth.
    Fine-tune the eyes

    The build reads these off the drawing; change one when it read wrong. Each needs a rebuild (from the eyes on). Shape and size are for irises the model has as pieces of their own, and an iris is never wider than its piece.

    3 · Mouth, then build
    How the mouth moves tracking · redrawn or free mouth · size
    Mouth tracking

    Makes the mouth follow yours in the tracking app: the jaw and the mouth's corners, not only the five talking shapes. Eye tracking and mouth tracking are separate: have both, either or neither.

    Mouth

    By default the talking mouth is redrawn on a patch of its own: it opens cleanly and never bends the face. Untick both to have the face itself bend around the marked mouth instead (the model's own mouth, as painted).

    Replaces the talking shapes (a, i, u, e, o) with drawn mouth shapes fitted to the marked mouth: a dark opening with a tongue that opens cleanly.

    Also paints the model's own mouth out of the texture and draws the whole mouth on a separate patch that moves on its own, so talking never bends the face. Best for simple or chibi faces, and when the face dents.

    how big the drawn mouth is against the marked one (1.3 = 30% bigger). On the picture in step 2, the solid shape is the mouth closed, where it rests, and the dashed outline is the widest it opens at this size; the outline follows the number as you change it. An estimate to choose the size by, without building.
    the points and every choice on this page save as you make them; the build is what puts them on the avatar

    Paint

    Fix the texture on the model itself: how this page works

    For a smudge by an eye, a seam of the wrong colour, a stray strand painted on the forehead, shading that is missing. Drag on the model to paint; drag beside it, or with the right button, to turn it; the middle button moves it; F (or Expand) gives the view the whole window.

    The paint is kept apart from the generated texture and laid over it when the avatar is built, so nothing here is final: the eraser brings the original back, and so does Clear. It goes on at the “build” step, so save and rebuild from there to see it on the avatar. Pick colours from the model (Alt+click) rather than by eye: a patch in the model's own colour is what makes a fix invisible. Isolate (H) shows one part alone, for places another part covers: the neck behind the hair, the inside of a collar. Keys: B brush, E eraser, S soften, C clone, G fill, I pick, H isolate / show all, F whole window, [ and ] brush size, Ctrl+Z undo, Shift+click a straight line from the last stroke.

    This character's face patch is an older kind Hair lying over the face and the face behind it share one picture in it, so paint on one shows on the other. Remaking the patch gives each its own (two Blender runs, about two minutes; the face points stay as they are, and paint already saved is kept).
    drag on the model: paint · drag beside it or right-drag: turn · scroll: zoom · middle-drag: move · F: whole window

    Step 7 of 8
    
    
    Movement 
    
    
    Catches clothing that doesn't move with the body, before you find it in your tracking app. Two tests: each runs in
      Blender for about a minute and then says here what it found, and what to do about it if anything. Nothing on this
      page moves on screen: to watch the avatar move, go on to Review.
    
  
    
  
    
    
    Pose test
    
      
    Bends the rig the way tracking apps do (arms raised, forward, out and down; head turned and nodded) and
        lists the pieces that don't come along: a sleeve left at the ribs, a hat that stays while the head turns. It
        suggests the part role that fixed each kind of problem before. About a minute; needs a build up to “pieces”.
    
      
    
    
    Physics test
    
      
    Runs the finished avatar's hair and cloth springs, standing still and then swaying, as tracking apps do
        (renders never run them). It reports hair or cloth that droops, pieces left behind by a swing, and swinging parts
        sinking into the body. Needs a finished build.

    More which Blender · what to bring · testing the app
    
    
    
    Which Blender runs the builds
    
      
    Any Blender 4.2 or later with the VRM Add-on ("VRM format" in Blender's Get Extensions). The app runs it in
        the background with only that add-on switched on, whatever else is installed in it, so your own Blender setup is
        never touched.
    
      
    BlenderWhereVRM Add-on
    
      
    
        
    
      
      
    
        
        
    
      
    Pick the blender.exe inside that Blender's folder. It is asked for its version (4.2 or later),
        added to the list above and used for builds from then on.
    
    
    
    
    What you need to bring
    
      
    A 3D model of your character. The tested way: generate it in Tripo standing in a
        T-pose (arms straight out), press Rig, pick "VRM 1.0 humanoid" and export as FBX. A plain
        .glb or .obj works too, and a finished .vrm (VRM 1.0) is improved as it is.
    
    
    
    
    Testing the app?
    
      
    Thank you. What helps most: build one character from start to finish, load the .vrm in
        your tracking app, and say what looked wrong and in which pose. When something breaks or looks
        off, open Stuck?, write what you saw and press the button: it gathers the settings, logs and pictures of
        that character into one folder. Send that folder (zipped) with the line below. The 3D model file itself is
        not in it; rendered pictures of the character and any screenshots you attached are.
