Model

A character is one model you turn into a VTuber avatar. Everything you decide on the later pages (parts, joints, face, options) is saved with it as you go, in out\<name>\character.json. To go back to one you already started, pick it in the Character list on the left.

Start a new character

The textured model straight from Tripo (P2 works best). Generate it standing in a T-pose (arms straight out) if you can: sleeves then don't fuse to the ribs. An A-pose (arms angled down) still builds.

A folder name for the build: letters, digits, _. Also the avatar's name in Warudo.

Not a second character: it's the same model with a skeleton Tripo already fitted. In Tripo, press Rig, pick "VRM 1.0 humanoid" and export as FBX (its GLB export is broken). Its skeleton and body weights usually bend better than the app's own. You can also add it later on this page.

Tripo's rig (optional)

The same model rigged in Tripo (Rig → "VRM 1.0 humanoid" → export FBX). With it, the skeleton and body weights come from Tripo instead of the app's own guess, which usually bends better at the shoulders and hips. Without it the app rigs the model itself; that works too.

Parts

A generated model is made of separate pieces (the body, hair strands, a hat, sleeves...). Here you say how each one should move. Most pieces are fine left on automatic; only give a role to what should move differently from the skin under it. Click a piece in the view or the list. Every change saves right away. Suggestions are guesses: a sleeve looks like cloth until an arm moves (the Movement page tests that).

What the roles do
Part of a piece for something fused into a bigger piece (a twin-tail that is part of the body): pick the piece, then paint the part with Alt+drag (Ctrl+Alt erases), or Shift+drag a box (Ctrl+Shift adds). Take all of it, ribbons and the hidden side included: whatever is left out stays on the old bone and tears away from the rest.

Joints

Where the skeleton's joints sit inside the model. Detection gets most models right; long robes and wide sleeves can fool it (a robe hem once read as the crotch, 44 cm off). Click a ball in the view (or a row in the list), then drag its arrows; it stays picked, so you can nudge it as often as you like. Each move saves on its own. Blue: detected. Orange: moved by you (Reset puts it back). Needs the piece listing (Parts).

Face

Tell the app where the eyes and mouth are, so it can make the avatar blink, talk and look around. Three steps, top to bottom. Left and right always mean the character's own sides (the character faces you, so their right eye is on your left).

1 · Face patch

2 · Mark the eyes and mouth

Click the highlighted point on the picture, twelve in all: four per eye (the two corners, the top and bottom of the opening, where the lashes meet the white) and four for the mouth. Drag a dot to move it. The small 3D view shows where each lands on the head: the dots must sit on the eyes and the mouth (a mouth once got marked on the nose). They save by themselves once all twelve are placed.

3 · How the face moves
Eye tracking

Makes the eyes follow where you look in the tracking app (Warudo: set eye movement to "blendshapes"). Blinking works either way.

Mouth

By default the mouth opens by bending the face around the marked mouth. If it barely opens, looks smeared, or the face dents when talking, redraw it.

Replaces the talking shapes (a, i, u, e, o) with drawn mouth shapes fitted to the marked mouth: a dark opening with a tongue that opens cleanly.

Also paints the model's own mouth out of the texture and draws the whole mouth on a separate patch that moves on its own, so talking never bends the face. Best for simple or chibi faces, and when the face dents.

how big the drawn mouth is against the marked one (1.3 = 30% bigger; model A used 1.3)

Options

Switches for the whole avatar. The defaults suit most models; "auto" lets the survey decide from what it saw, and says what it picked. Each change saves on its own. After changing any, rebuild (Build page).

Build

Turns everything you set up into the avatar file (.vrm). Build everything runs the whole chain, 10–20 minutes. Each step is one Blender run and keeps its own files; later steps read what the step before them made, so rerunning a step marks the ones after it stale. One run at a time. Grey steps are switched off, and the note says why.

Movement

  
Catches clothing that doesn't move with the body, before you find it in Warudo. Two tests, both run in
    Blender and report back; neither animates here. To watch the avatar move, open it in the viewer at the bottom.

  

    
Pose test
      
Bends the rig the way tracking apps do (arms raised, forward, out and down; head turned and nodded) and
        lists the pieces that don't come along: a sleeve left at the ribs, a hat that stays while the head turns. It
        suggests the part role that fixed each kind of problem before. About a minute; needs a build up to “pieces”.

      

    
Physics test
      
Runs the finished avatar's hair and cloth springs, standing still and then swaying, as Warudo does
        (renders never run them). It reports hair or cloth that droops when standing still, pieces left behind by a swing,
        and swinging parts sinking into the body. Needs a finished build.

Stuck?

  
Something looks wrong and no part role or option fixes it. Say what you see; this gathers the manifest, checks, logs and renders into one folder for Claude Code (or any agent) to pick up. The agent drives the same build with python -m app.
